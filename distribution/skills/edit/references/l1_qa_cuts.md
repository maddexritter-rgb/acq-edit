# L1 Q&A: raw interaction to reviewable cuts

Use when a host works through a business owner's problem and the input is raw footage.
Natural invocation: `/edit <source> — L1 Q&A, cuts only`.
This is a mode of `edit`, not a second cutting, reframing, or caption skill.
If the input is an editor-selected draft, use [l1_draft_finishing.md](l1_draft_finishing.md)
and preserve its selection unless the user explicitly requests new editorial cuts.

For concrete selection tradeoffs, see [worked editorial comparisons](l1_qa_cuts_worked_examples.md).
These include a useful compression, a revision failure, and a reference fidelity risk.

## Editorial contract

Deliver a useful, intelligible interaction with minimal human correction.
→ Use skill: `assembly-design`; its [L1 short-form calibration](../../assembly-design/references/profiles.md#l1-short-form-calibration--2026-10-26)
owns duration, cold-viewer hook clarity, early banter and candidate comparison for vertical shorts.
There is no mandatory seven-beat pattern, revenue-first hook, host-within-five-seconds
rule, word-count floor, or ban on reordering. These scoped rules supersede conflicting
legacy Q&A prompts and regex scores. Existing source-value and delivery checks remain.

Before selecting, read the entire interaction through its actual resolution and the
surrounding handoff. Identify the owner's stated question, the problem the dialogue
actually reveals, and the answer the viewer will receive. These can differ. A decision
framework or personal tradeoff can be the reward; do not manufacture a growth tactic.
Do not use the first thanks as an automatic interaction boundary. Continue through the
handoff to check for a host coda, correction, reassurance, objection, or audience exchange
that completes or changes the selected promise; stop before the next owner's interaction.
A next-owner greeting does not terminate the prior audit when the host immediately resumes
the prior topic. Inventory that interleaved coda before deciding whether it belongs to the
selected promise, qualifies a separate branch, or can be removed. The interaction boundary
must follow topic completion, not speaker order alone.

State what this cut answers: the consultation, one useful sub-answer, a standalone story,
or a human exchange. Match the opening promise to that scope. Check later facts before
claiming an early principle solved the owner's actual problem.
Preserve the L1 Q&A format when it carries value. Do not replace a strong owner-specific
problem, measurable desire and applied recommendation with a host-only anecdote merely
because the anecdote is shorter and self-contained. Default to the strongest complete
owner-specific sub-answer. Select a standalone host story only when its teaching payoff
clearly outranks the available owner-specific answer, and record that comparison.
A host-led framework is not host-only when the source explicitly applies it back to the
owner's retained problem. Rank candidates by which one resolves the actual bottleneck,
not by how many lines the owner speaks. A location-demand tactic does not outrank a team-
speed tool when the owner's stated obstacle is dragging the team and the later framework
contains a concrete “for you” application to that obstacle.
When later measurements produce a more specific diagnosis, treat that diagnosis as its
own candidate. Either keep the earlier principle narrow without borrowing the later final
prescription, or select the later diagnosis and retain the measurements that earn it. Do
not attach a final directive to an early explanation when intervening facts changed why
the directive follows.

Before selecting words, make a branch ledger for the full interaction. List each
substantive diagnosis, objection, alternative, example branch, correction and final
recap; mark whether it completes, qualifies, reverses or sits outside the selected
promise. Choose the strongest developed promise with a useful payoff. Do not combine the
opening of one branch, the mechanism of another and the reaction to a third merely
because each moment is individually strong.

Rank the viable promises before choosing the shortest one. A surprising measurement,
correction or diagnosis is setup unless the viewer also learns what it means or what to
do with it. When the raw continuation interprets the number, tests it against the offer,
or reaches a source-grounded action, prefer that complete value chain over a shorter cut
that merely ends on the datum. Completeness includes the context, personality and
development that make the strongest useful promise worth watching, not only a technically
defensible stopping point.

Do not confuse fidelity with retaining the full discovery history. When the clean answer
can use a later direct confirmation, keep that confirmation instead of the longer mistaken
premise, correction and reconciliation. For example, if a full pipeline came from referrals
but direct mail separately produced deals, either retain that distinction or omit the
pipeline result and use the clean direct-mail confirmation. Never place a result beside a
channel, offer or action when the raw conversation attributes that result elsewhere.

If a proposed sub-answer runs beyond twice the working target, rerank and narrow it before
accepting a duration exception. Look for repeated setup, full diagnostic history, parallel
proof and an extended hypothetical that can be replaced by one clean source-grounded
confirmation or qualification. A long exception is warranted only when the selected
mechanism itself is irreducible; documenting that the interaction has many relevant
branches is not enough.

Use `consultation` only when the cut resolves the governing interaction through every
material answer branch and late objection. Most useful extractions from a multi-branch
exchange are `sub_answer`s. If the branch ledger says the claim was narrowed, the plan
cannot call itself a complete consultation.

Name the promise boundary more precisely when the distinction changes what must remain:

| Selected promise | Do not silently expand it into |
|---|---|
| A readiness test or decision criterion | A complete implementation guide |
| An entry/acquisition offer diagnosis | A diagnosis of the next sale or cross-sell |
| A channel or partner-acquisition lesson | A system for activating dormant partners |
| A sourcing or conversion diagnosis | A complete operating system for the function |

For each promise, preserve the customer or actor, the relevant sales/decision moment,
and the action or evidence that completes that specific answer. A separate unanswered
follow-up does not invalidate a complete sub-answer, but it does limit what the short can claim.

Match the operating level of the payoff to the opening. If the opening promises that a
founder can leave frontline work, an individual coaching loop is incomplete until the
source explains who inherits that loop and how it transfers to leaders. The bridge may
be a concrete example: leaders demonstrate the behavior, then the owner reinforces the
leaders for coaching others. Check the retained actor/action chain before restoring an
omitted abstract explanation. If that transfer is absent, retain a source-grounded
bridge or narrow the opening to shaping one person's behavior. Apply the same check
when a question moves from individual to team, team to organization, or operator to
owner: a complete lower-level method does not automatically resolve the higher-level promise.

For a hiring, partner or channel recommendation, retain both sides of the match: why the
candidate/channel can perform and why the opportunity is attractive or applicable here.
A label such as “hire an agency owner” is not a complete mechanism when the useful lesson
is the exchange of skills, economics or advantage that makes the match work.

Design backward from that reward. Preserve the necessary context, reasoning, constraints,
and concrete example that let a cold viewer understand why it follows. Then choose the
opening from real source words that establish the dilemma. Personal stakes, a contrast,
or a later explicit question may work; do not force every opening into the same formula.
When the reward is the owner's choice between paths, retain the decision device that
earned that choice. Preserve both alternatives in enough specificity to compare them,
the criterion used to decide, the owner's actual selection, and any staged next step.
A later choice cannot substitute for an omitted goal or one-sided comparison.

For an opening, keep the minimum source-grounded combination that identifies the business
or situation, surfaces the consequence/choice/desire, and preserves any uncertainty or
constraint that changes the answer. Revenue, profit, family context and personality earn
space only when they change the advice, quantify the stakes or make the selected story
intelligible. A later consequence may move before the original question when its referent
and business identity remain clear, but uncertainty must stay uncertain, hypothetical
results must stay hypothetical, and a corrected assertion cannot outlive its correction.
A later generalized restatement may replace owner-specific setup only when it preserves
the exact degree and qualification of the problem. If the owner corrects “stops” to
“slows,” keep that correction or use the owner-specific opening; broader applicability
does not authorize a stronger premise.
Cold-read the first 15–20 seconds against the actual payoff before accepting the opening.

Remove diagnostic detours, duplicate explanations, unneeded setup detail, and conversational
tails before polishing individual words. Keep a numerical example when it makes the
advice actionable. Keep a story or reaction when it completes the same idea; do not
delete it solely because it is a second example or another speaker.

Live audience corroboration earns space when it directly neutralizes the owner's stated
objection. A concise practitioner answer about how quickly a skill is taught can replace
an extended personal anecdote when the selected promise is attainability or learnability.
Treat that answer as evidence, not as the owner's reply or an independently verified
estimate. Corroboration cannot replace a causal dependency required by a broader growth
promise. If conversion, capacity, or implementation conditions are omitted, narrow the
promise to learning the skill or regaining control of the function.

Design the ending as the completion of the selected promise. Prefer a source-grounded
order of operations, decision criterion, conditional fork, completed tactic, relevant
precedent or memorable summary of an already-explained mechanism. A motivational line
without its mechanism is not a payoff. Before accepting the ending, read the omitted raw
continuation for a material objection, correction or condition. Thanks or agreement may
move only when they still respond to the retained answer and do not imply an omitted
concern was resolved. Preserve qualifiers on projected results and every trigger/action
needed by a tactical checklist. A sentence-terminal source word can still be an
idea-incomplete ending; acoustic completeness is a separate boundary gate.
Conversely, a phrase such as “I was like—” may introduce a performed reaction. Inspect
the picture and sound beyond the last transcript token before trimming: a gesture,
expression or acted pause can complete the payoff. Retain it for its demonstrated
function, not for a fixed tail duration; absent ASR words do not prove disposable silence.
An analogy or transferred example after a completed tactic earns the extra runtime only
when the retained dialogue explicitly maps it back to the selected actor, problem or
decision. If its callback or application is omitted, end at the prior complete example
instead of letting the short drift into a second unresolved subject.

Before removing a short reply or branch, check its function:

| Function | Preserve when shortening |
|---|---|
| Corrects an assertion | The correction, or remove the incorrect assertion too. An emotional label does not replace a factual retraction. |
| Changes the recommendation | The fact that explains the change, such as an action the owner already tried. |
| Defines a conditional choice | The goal, prerequisite or alternative that changes which advice applies. |
| Explains a tactic | Its relevant actor, offer, payer/payee, order, deadline trigger and required action. |
| Limits certainty | Enough qualification to keep an estimate hypothetical; redundant hesitation can go if uncertainty still survives. |

Use only the relevant checks. When multiple businesses appear, trace which business each
question and answer concerns. Do not introduce one business and silently attach another's
advice. A personality opening or callback earns space only alongside the setup its payoff
needs. Check the assembled dialogue, not just whether all planned beats have labels.
After removing a branch, check whether its setup, repeated question, or later clarification
still serves the retained dialogue. Preserve the reply's meaning while removing dependencies
that disappeared with the branch.

Audit deictic words after every deletion or move: “this,” “that,” “here,” “it,” “those
two things,” and similar phrases must still point to one clear retained referent. Shared
topic is insufficient when the original phrase referred to a specific step, option,
number, objection or demonstration.
When the referent is a board, gesture or graphic, record that visual dependency in the
finishing handoff and verify it remains understandable in the rendered cut. A source-
supported label can clarify the referent without changing the spoken words; the label
does not certify what was said or turn an illustrative score into measured data.

Reordering may clarify the existing argument. For each backward source move, explain the
original relationship and why the new adjacency preserves it. Preserve speaker identity,
negations, uncertainty, money/time units, corrections, and causal/conditional links. Never
turn a hypothetical into a fact, a later clarification into prior knowledge, or a question
about feelings into an accusation. If a move needs invented words, redesign with source.

Check short responses against their original question. A verbatim “yes,” “no,” or
“I don't know” can change meaning when moved. Record what the response originally
answered and what the new placement implies; use a faithful source exchange if those
meanings differ. Apply the same check to reaction shots and apparent agreement. A later
summary can introduce the method if its meaning holds, but a moved acknowledgment must
not imply that the shorter answer resolved every omitted concern.

A changed-function uncertainty line can create a truthful comic contradiction only when
the same short quickly reveals the speaker's actual specific answer and the moved line is
not used as evidence for the recommendation. Record it as a tone/reconstruction choice,
not as a faithful question-response pair. For automatic selection, prefer the equally
strong chronological version; use the comic reconstruction only when its engagement gain
clearly earns the added meaning risk.

For reused numbers, preserve the metric, time unit, original question and certainty.
If the speaker explicitly corrects a material number or unit, use the corrected meaning;
a source-grounded splice can compress the stumble, subject to acoustic verification.
A coincidentally matching number from another answer is not interchangeable provenance.
If exact values are abstracted into labels such as “high ticket,” “low ticket,” “large,”
or “small,” preserve which named offer, customer and decision each value originally
described. A cleaner label is a fidelity error when it swaps the compared alternatives.

When removing an advice branch, judge the claim that remains. A discarded alternative
may take its explanation with it, but retain the facts and qualifications needed by the
selected recommendation. Do not turn case-specific advice into an unconditional rule.

## Execution

1. **Establish sources.** Reuse verified local media. Inventory dependencies and free space
   before acquiring more; download only the next useful missing session. Record camera,
   audio, transcript, and source-clock identities in the job. Never infer sync from names.
   → Use skill: `source-intel`; use `long-form-ingest` for missing transcription.
2. **Read full context.** Follow [editorial_design.md](editorial_design.md) for coverage
   and source-grounded value anchors. Both voices must be intelligible in the transcript;
   a host-only mic transcript cannot represent the owner's question. Correct consequential
   ASR errors against audio before treating the affected passage as a verified example.
3. **Write the selection.** Save a source-indexed plan using
   [l1_cut_plan_schema.md](l1_cut_plan_schema.md). Record why each kept span earns its
   place and account for the removed source ranges. Use the current v6 plan and cite
   retained word ranges for every opening, promise and completion claim. Use v6's ranked
   promise and format reviews plus its branch ledger to account for substantive answers
   and qualifications; review the compiler's
   quoted evidence rather than trusting the model's summary. Resolve every required
   mid-sentence boundary review by preserving a complete edited phrase. Read the actual
   assembled words as a cold viewer; a plausible outline is insufficient. Do not use `cut_design.py` unchanged:
   its legacy prompt/backend and no-reordering constraints do not implement this mode.
4. **Compile and inspect.** Run `scripts/l1_cut_plan.py`. `STRUCTURE_VALID` verifies
   source identity, word membership, declared anchors, and exhaustive range accounting.
   It does not establish good judgment, correct speakers, safe seams, or a finished edit.
   Resolve every semantic discontinuity before treating the plan as a build candidate.
5. **Resolve real cuts.** → Use skill: `assembly-design`. Specify boundary-only refinement versus
   internal cleanup, including deliberately kept pauses/reactions. Compiler times are rough ASR selections,
   never frame-accurate edit points. After resolving boundaries, write the actual
   `design_evidence.json`, run `scripts/design_evidence.py`, and reread the assembled words.
6. **Build and review.** Follow RUNBOOK's native Premiere assembly and live-readback
   contract. Reuse one project and one `01_CUTS` sequence; original media remains editable.
   → Use skill: `audit-script`, assembly review mode, with its required blind review
   packet. Keep full-source fidelity checking with the builder; do not supply the fresh
   reviewer with the builder's reasoning or prior verdict. Fix cited deficiencies.
   Existing acoustic and assembly-handoff gates must pass before calling cuts ready.
7. **After approval.** → Use skill: `horizontal-to-vertical` for framing/camera treatment;
   → Use skill: `caption-clips` only through its native editable path when editability is
   required. Follow `l1_draft_finishing.md` and RUNBOOK for finishing. Do not silently
   burn in captions or grade/graphics, or start finishing during a cuts-only request.

## Learning from reference projects

Keep the case library in the job/brand's own folder, supplied by path. Do not put client
names, personal absolute paths, media, credentials, or project-specific offsets here.
For each case record project hash, sequence UID, source files, transcript hash, stage
lineage, original interaction bounds, and output ranges. Names such as RAW, DRAFT,
FINAL are clues, not proof of stage: scratch edits, nests, and stray layers can inflate
duration. Verify the actual content and dependencies before forming an input/target pair.
Use project hash plus full title/path as the case identity. Numeric clip IDs can be reused
for unrelated projects; never merge examples, evidence, or lessons by number alone.

Identify the delivered asset/version independently of sequence labels. Reconcile source
recorder time, inner draft time, outer sequence trims, export start timecode, and acquired
media time. Measure offsets for each file; a player proxy may differ from the export.
Check both sides of revision points before applying one offset. Later stages may restore
context, change internal joins or merely shorten a tail. A shorter duration alone does
not prove tighter dialogue; a later version label does not prove the selected assembly.
Do not interpret a clock mismatch or an outer-nest trim as the editor deleting words.

Map **audible dialogue** to source, including enabled tracks, nests, J/L edits, gain/mute
state, and alternate/enhanced audio. Picture-only source maps are provisional evidence:
a reaction shot does not prove dialogue was cut. Quantify unmapped regions and inspect
them; do not call a partly mapped case a frame-accurate gold label.
When extracting Premiere text evidence, resolve shared payloads, referenced master text,
and component state before treating an empty local XML element as empty content. A hidden
negation or qualifier in shared text can reverse the apparent editorial decision. Text
recovery still does not prove that the element was active, visible, or audible.

ASR token differences such as “gonna” versus “going to,” collapsed repetitions, or
missing acknowledgments require phrase/audio reconciliation. An unmatched token is
not proof of deletion, and one source token cannot certify multiple delivered words.
For a consequential reply or phrase missing from mixed ASR, inspect the isolated speaker
source and corresponding export. Compare acronym/contraction phrases as groups. Detect
collapsed word timings before using them: a regional re-transcription can supply a better
candidate, but not certified phoneme edges. Preserve original ASR and attach evidence;
strongly matching sound can receive different transcripts, even across repeated runs.
Forced alignment needs text faithful to the exact audio channel being aligned. Mixed
transcripts can omit cross-talk or substitute the other speaker. Reject alignments that
contradict independently mapped source spans, even when the aligner exits successfully;
a shorter ASR crop is not automatically more accurate.
Track provenance, editorial meaning, and acoustic boundary verification separately.
Distinguish:

- Observed decisions: retained/removed/moved source ranges with verified provenance.
- Editorial interpretation: why a choice appears to improve this particular idea.
- Hypothesis: a reusable rule still needing tests on other interactions and shoots.

Choose development examples across dilemma types and lengths. Reserve entire unseen
sessions before reading their reference edits, and keep alternate versions of the same
interaction in the same split. A studied example is not a holdout. For evaluation, give
the agent raw footage, this mode, and the normal shared skills; freeze its proposed cut
before opening the editor's reference. Compare context, fidelity, reasoning, reward,
seams, and the editor's actual correction effort. Exact timeline imitation is not the goal.

Promote a rule only after reviewing failures and counterexamples. Do not claim broad
reliability from structural tests, a handful of examples, or agreement with a reference
whose audio remains unresolved. Record the model and version for each evaluation.
