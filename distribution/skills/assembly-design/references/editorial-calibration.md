# Editorial construction calibration

Use this for batches, mixed workshop/event footage, and retries after an assembly looked
like raw in/out windows rather than edited clips.

## What good looks like

A completed assembly is a designed unit of value:

1. The opening makes a specific source-supported promise. In Q&A, the audience or owner
   question stays when it supplies the condition that makes the answer valuable.
2. The middle retains the reasoning ladder, mechanism, concrete example or story that
   earns the conclusion. A title or hook overlay cannot replace this work.
3. The last retained line resolves the opening promise with an outcome, decision,
   principle, story button or concrete next step. It does not merely stop speaking.
4. Every retained turn has a function. Remove greetings, false starts, repeated versions,
   abandoned constructions, detached backchannels, unrelated branches and wind-downs
   when they do not serve this promise.
5. Reordering is available when source meaning, chronology, speaker knowledge and
   question-response relationships survive. The natural source order is not privileged.
6. A contiguous passage is valid only when it already performs all five jobs cleanly.
   Do not manufacture jump cuts; equally, do not call an untouched excerpt "designed."

## Calibrate before scaling

If the user supplies an approved or strongly preferred prior batch, read its exact
dialogue plans, user revisions and final native cut—not merely a summary. Record which
decisions generalize and which were specific to that source. Design the first three new
clips, compare their unedited and selected dialogue, and inspect them as a calibration
set before scaling to the rest of the source.

Do not design dozens of clips in one generation. Work in groups of no more than five,
with a separate editorial decision and comparison artifact per clip. Batch automation
begins only after the dialogue decisions are frozen.

## Failure signatures

Stop and redesign when any of these appear:

- plan fields repeat the same explanation across different clips;
- the candidate title changes but hook/payoff rationale does not;
- most clips equal their raw candidate windows with only head/tail trimming;
- a retained example repeats value without adding needed explanation, contrast or evidence;
- source context was narrowed to conceal missing qualifications or improve a keep ratio;
- a clip runs far past what its format normally needs — treat as a scope symptom to
  investigate, never as a cue to trim words down to a target length;
- unsafe seams are "fixed" by restoring all intervening material;
- a Q&A answer loses the question or condition that tells the viewer why it applies;
- a payoff is a topic-ending sentence but does not reward the opening promise;
- a reviewer receives the builder's verdict or its report is authored from a template;
- a large batch reaches Premiere before a small calibration set is inspected.

The correct response is not to demand more cuts. Return to the complete interaction,
redesign the spoken arc, and let the necessary number of cuts follow from that decision.

Keep ratios, whole-file selections and single spans are inspection signals only, never
failure criteria. A complete scripted take can pass unchanged. A necessary multi-example
explanation can stay together. Human approval is recorded separately from every gate.

Validate new routes on a small pilot, then two consecutive unseen batches of at most five.
Do not give test editors the desired answer or failed-run rationale. Require human
confirmation of minor-adjustment-only quality before calling the route validated.
